# Crit 8 reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The breakthrough was turning my idea for a game into a workflow I could follow.
I knew the experience I wanted: powerful weapons, different vehicles, and players
fighting and building together. Talking with the agent helped me get technical
perspectives, refine those ideas and decide what to build first. Making one
playable stage gave me something concrete to try and respond to. That made the
next step easier to see.

Reusing parts became a useful example of how to keep moving. The HE, incendiary
and gas shell types could provide a starting point for later weapons, with
different delivery methods and effects. We could build on earlier work instead
of deciding everything again. Progress felt smoother through this cycle of
discussion, implementation and trying the result, although my feedback still
uncovered problems such as rail turns and unclear fire and gas effects.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

I realised that I need to understand more of the whole development pipeline.
I am still learning how a game is structured, what runs in the browser, what
the server controls, and how progress is saved. Working with the agent means
I need to explain my intentions clearly and understand enough of the architecture
to judge its suggestions.

I want to become better at breaking a large idea into manageable stages,
recognising reusable parts and checking how they fit together. This project
gives me a practical way to learn those skills while building something I enjoy.
